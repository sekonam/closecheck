# CloseCheck — promotion & channel research

> **Link correction (2026-09-22):** `selffundedsearchconference.com` now redirects to an unrelated site; the conference's working domain is `selffundedsearch.org`.


**Date: 2026-09-21. Scope: concrete, current (2025–2026) promotion channels for launch — where the
two target populations (tech-DD providers on marketplaces; QoE generalists / IT consultants who
could add a tech-diligence line) actually congregate, what each channel costs, and what the first
90 days post-launch should look like. Builds on `distribution.md` (2026-09-11); does not modify it.**

Confidence notation follows the house style: `EVIDENCE` (directly fetched/quoted), `INFERENCE`
(reasoned from evidence), `ASSUMPTION` (flagged, needs verification).

## 1. The big thing `distribution.md` missed: the ETA/searcher ecosystem

The 2026-09-11 pass looked at trade associations (IBBA, AM&AA, ACG) and DueDilio, and concluded the
buyer population had to be assembled manually. What it never examined is the **entrepreneurship-
through-acquisition (ETA) / searcher ecosystem** — a dense, highly online community where SMB
buyers *and the consultants who serve them* both congregate. This ecosystem matters to CloseCheck
in two distinct ways:

1. **Consultants market themselves there.** SMB-focused QoE firms sponsor searcher conferences,
   guest on searcher podcasts, and publish in searcher newsletters — because that's where their
   clients are. The Self-Funded Search Conference's public sponsor list includes QoE/financial-
   diligence and back-office firms (Lummis Consulting Services, System Six, Live Oak Bank)
   (`selffundedsearch.org/events` / `selffundedsearchconference.com`, retrieved 2026-09-21,
   `EVIDENCE`). A vendor present in these venues is visible to exactly the provider population
   CloseCheck sells to — and can read the sponsor/speaker lists as a *named prospect list*.
2. **Demand-side pull is possible without violating the "consultant is the customer" guardrail.**
   Buyers (searchers) who learn "your QoE doesn't cover the tech stack" ask their QoE provider about
   it. Marketing *messages* into buyer communities that create the question, while selling the
   *product* to the consultant who answers it, is consistent with `docs/spec.md` §1–2 — the buyer
   remains the end-reader/end-beneficiary, the consultant remains the customer. This is a lever
   `distribution.md` never priced.

## 2. DueDilio re-checked: still not a lead list, but there is now one warm named door

- `duedilio.com/partner-program/`, retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE`: the partner program is
  **unchanged since the 2026-09-11 pass** — a referral program for people who send DueDilio deal
  flow (advisors, brokers, attorneys, lenders, "platforms, communities, and ETA educator
  programs"), $149 platform fee paid by the referred client, and **"no mention of technology vendor
  partnerships or software tools for providers."** `distribution.md`'s core negative finding stands:
  DueDilio is category proof, not a contactable list, and there is no vendor track to apply to.
- **New finding: DueDilio's founder is personally reachable through his own media property.** Roman
  Beylin writes *The Business Inquirer* (thebusinessinquirer.substack.com), a curated SMB M&A
  newsletter with **3,800+ subscribers** covering "articles, online discussions, events, tools, and
  personal insights," aimed at "searchers, independent sponsors, private equity professionals"
  (about page, retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE`). It explicitly covers *tools*. `INFERENCE`: a
  well-made free sample audit pitched to Beylin as newsletter material is simultaneously (a) a
  cheap, credible media placement in front of the exact ecosystem, and (b) the soft opening of the
  eventual DueDilio relationship that `distribution.md` deferred until 3–5 paying customers exist —
  without asking DueDilio for anything its anti-disintermediation model protects.

## 3. The named-list problem is smaller than `distribution.md` thought

`distribution.md` concluded the provider list had to be assembled the hard way (associations,
LinkedIn search). Mostly true — but the **SMB-focused QoE segment self-promotes publicly**, unlike
DueDilio's anonymized cards. One search session surfaced, by name, with websites: Guardian Due
Diligence, Rapid Diligence, Centurica, Bedrock QoE, WebAcquisition, Midwest CPA, Builders CPA,
Treewalk, Eton Venture Services — plus aggregator/comparison content that names more
(ctacquisitions.com's "18 Firms Ranked by Price & Sector," thewebsiteflip.com's QoE provider guide,
projectionhub.com's provider list; all retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE`). These comparison guides
are effectively pre-built prospect directories for population #2 (QoE generalists who don't do tech
diligence today). `INFERENCE`: a first-pass named list of 60–100 SMB-serving diligence firms is a
few hours' work from public sources, not the multi-week association-mining exercise
`distribution.md` budgeted. The free-audit-hook outreach motion it designed transfers unchanged —
it just starts from a better list.

## 4. Content/SEO: the SMB angle of the SERP is empty (the gap distribution.md couldn't check)

`distribution.md` flagged the SERP check as an unresolved gap. Partial resolution this session:

- The pages ranking for "technology due diligence checklist" are **all enterprise/software-deal
  oriented**: DealRoom, M&A Science, AKF Partners, RingStone ("85 areas"), L40, Performance
  Improvement Partners, mnacommunity.com (search results retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE` of who
  ranks). Every one assumes the target has an engineering org, a codebase, a CTO. **None address
  main-street reality** — POS systems, Google Business Profile ownership, domain/DNS control,
  booking-platform contracts, license transferability — CloseCheck's actual scope.
- Exact search volumes could not be verified without a keyword tool (`ASSUMPTION`; the head term is
  plausibly low-hundreds-to-low-thousands/month, and SMB long-tail variants — "due diligence buying
  a restaurant website," "tech checklist buying a small business" — individually tiny).
- `INFERENCE`: the opportunity is not to outrank RingStone on the head term but to **own the
  currently unserved SMB long tail** with 2–3 cornerstone pieces plus a downloadable Tier-A-shaped
  checklist as lead magnet. Low competition, but low volume and 6+ month payoff — a background
  channel, not a launch channel. The DealStream "Website Due Diligence Checklist" page is the
  closest existing SMB-adjacent content and it's thin.

## 5. Comparable GTM case studies (the evidence `distribution.md` couldn't find)

Three documented cases of niche vertical SaaS selling to professional-services practitioners:

- **Clio (legal practice management):** "co-developed their MVP with over 200 law firms" and won GTM
  via "targeted content, CLE-eligible webinars, and strategic legal conference sponsorships"
  (swarnendu.de vertical-SaaS analysis, retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE` of the account,
  secondary source). Lesson: education content that carries *professional-practice value* (for
  CloseCheck: "how to add a tech-diligence line item to your QoE engagement," webinar or template
  pack) beats product marketing with this buyer type.
- **Jetpack Workflow (accounting workflow):** pre-sold by interviewing "hundreds of firms," then
  built its own podcast (*Growing Your Firm*) as the primary distribution channel — interviewing
  prospects *is* the marketing (technical.ly profile + jetpackworkflow.com, retrieved 2026-09-21,
  `EVIDENCE`, secondary source). Directly copyable: every free sample audit and every outreach
  conversation is also content.
- **Auxpanel (dental compliance):** bootstrapped to 100+ practices at $300–500/month with "zero
  marketing budget" via word-of-mouth in a tight vertical (medium.com vertical-SaaS piece,
  retrieved 2026-09-21, `ASSUMPTION` — single unverified secondary source, treat as directional).
- Common pattern across all three, `INFERENCE`: first ~100 customers came from founder-led,
  community-embedded motions (interviews, education, conference presence in the vertical's own
  venues) — not from paid acquisition. This retroactively *evidences* `distribution.md` §2's
  reasoned-but-unevidenced conclusion.

## 6. Paid channels: priced, and mostly deferred

- **LinkedIn ads:** 2026 benchmarks for financial-services/finance-professional targeting: **CPC
  $7–15, CPL $100–300** (stackmatix.com, theb2bhouse.com, get-ryze.ai benchmark guides, retrieved
  2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE` for benchmarks). `INFERENCE`: with a total addressable audience of perhaps
  a few thousand SMB-diligence professionals, cold LinkedIn ads are the wrong instrument at launch —
  the audience is small enough to reach one-by-one for free. Narrow retargeting (site visitors,
  checklist downloaders) becomes reasonable once traffic exists; budget ≤$500/month if tried.
- **Podcast sponsorship:** viable and cheap by B2B standards (see §1 — expect ~$1,000–2,500/episode
  flat for the ETA shows, `INFERENCE` from benchmark CPMs). But **guesting is free** and these shows
  book guests constantly; sponsor only after a guest spot or two proves message-market fit.
- **Newsletter sponsorship:** likely the cheapest paid test available — small SMB-M&A newsletters
  at 4–40k subscribers typically price placements in the low hundreds to ~$1,500 (`ASSUMPTION` —
  none of the three publish a rate card publicly; ask directly).
- **Conference sponsorship:** Main Street Summit from $1,000 (`EVIDENCE`); SFSC not-for-profit, so
  plausibly low-thousands (`ASSUMPTION`); NAGGL (SBA lender ecosystem) registration alone $2,095
  (`EVIDENCE`, naggl.org) with sponsorship above that — defer NAGGL entirely, the lender path to
  consultants is second-order.
- **Accounting-alliance vendor programs:** BDO Alliance USA (250+ CPA firms) runs a real **Vendor
  Marketing Program** with recent SaaS precedent (Netgain accepted, PR Newswire 2025/2026)
  (bdo.com + prnewswire.com, retrieved 2026-09-21, `EVIDENCE`). This reaches population #2 (QoE
  generalists inside CPA firms) at scale, but is a slow, application-based, relationship-heavy
  channel — a year-two motion, recorded here so it isn't forgotten.

## Recommended first-90-days sequence (post-launch)

Assumes launch ≈ early October 2026, founder-led, budget ≤$3,000 cash total.

**Weeks 1–2 — list + baseline assets.**
Build the 60–100-firm named list from §3 sources (QoE comparison guides, firm sites, SFSC sponsor
list, LinkedIn search on "technology due diligence"/"IT diligence" headlines). Produce one polished
free Tier-A sample audit on a real live listing (the shareable artifact everything else reuses).
Start the distribution.md week-1 experiment: 20 LinkedIn messages vs. 20 cold emails to matched
prospects; measure reply rates.

**Weeks 1–4 — free community presence (parallel).**
Join Searchfunder and EBIT community; publish one genuinely useful post ("what financial QoE
doesn't check: a tech-diligence gap analysis on a real listing" — the sample audit, reframed).
Begin a low-cadence SMB X presence. Pitch Roman Beylin the sample audit as Business Inquirer
material (tool feature or paid placement, whichever he offers).

**Weeks 3–8 — outreach at volume + podcast pitches.**
Work the list at 15–20 contacts/week using whichever channel won the A/B, always leading with the
free-audit-on-your-live-deal offer. Pitch guest spots: Acquisitions Anonymous (deal-teardown-with-
tech-angle format fits their show) and 2–3 QoE/diligence-adjacent shows; expect airing in months
2–3. Run one paid newsletter test (~$200–500) in whichever of SMB Deal Hunter / Business Inquirer
responds.

**Week ~6 (Nov 7–9) — Self-Funded Search Conference, Dallas, as attendee.**
Priority targets: the diligence-firm sponsors and any QoE professionals speaking on the diligence
panels. Goal: 10+ real conversations with named firms already on the outreach list.

**Weeks 8–12 — convert signal into the durable layer.**
If outreach reply rate <5% after ~60 contacts, buy the IBBA Supplier Directory listing ($595–895)
per distribution.md's trigger. Publish 2–3 SMB-angle SEO cornerstone pieces + downloadable
checklist lead magnet (starts the 6-month clock while momentum exists). Compile channel-by-channel
results; decide whether month 4+ escalates to podcast sponsorship (~$1–2.5k/ep) and SMBash 2027 /
conference sponsorship — gated, per distribution.md, on having 3–5 nameable customers.

**Explicitly not in the first 90 days:** LinkedIn cold ads, NAGGL/SBA-lender motion, BDO Alliance
application, any formal DueDilio partnership pitch (the Beylin newsletter touch is the only DueDilio-
adjacent move), conference *sponsorship* of any kind.
