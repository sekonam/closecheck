# Competitor landscape refresh — CloseCheck

**Retrieved 2026-09-21.** Refresh of [`competitors.md`](competitors.md) (retrieved 2026-09-11). Same
framing: B2B SaaS selling structured technology-DD audit workflows (three-tier checklist engine,
whitelabeled report generation, magic-link client portal, business-type template library) to
M&A/QoE consultants advising buyers of $200k–2M small businesses. Sold to the **provider**, not the
deal-side buyer.

Unlike the 2026-09-11 baseline (which ran on a proxy workaround after `WebSearch` exhaustion), this
pass used full `WebSearch` + direct `WebFetch` on vendor pages. Claims below marked `EVIDENCE`
(retrieved directly), `SECONDARY` (from a third-party roundup, not the vendor's own page), or
`INFERENCE`.

## 1. What changed since competitors.md (delta section)

1. **DueDilio shipped a fully self-service platform in 2026.** The baseline's §4 flagged DueDilio as
   "motive and means, no visible move — evidence of timing, not incapacity." The timing has now
   moved: DueDilio's own about page records a 2026 launch of a "fully self-service M&A services
   marketplace" (browse vetted providers, submit projects, compare proposals side-by-side, message,
   hire in-platform). `EVIDENCE` (duedilio.com/about, duedilio.com homepage, 2026-09-21). Still **no
   provider-facing tooling, no AI features, no productized diligence** anywhere on the site — but
   they have now demonstrably built transactional software, which was the missing capability signal.
   Scale: 1,300+ projects, 1,100+ clients, 4.9★ Trustpilot, $149 lifetime buyer fee (unchanged).
2. **An SMB-buyer-facing AI diligence category has visibly formed.** Not present in the baseline at
   all. At least five 2025–2026 entrants sell AI-driven diligence directly to searchers/micro-PE/
   self-funded buyers of small businesses: **DEALPRINT** (AI-native diligence platform, sub-$50M
   deals, red-flag detection, "knowledge packs" for owner dependency / licensing / customer
   concentration, Individual/Team/Fund tiers, positioned against $30–75k QoE firms), **Devaland
   "Deal OS"** (reads CIM + financials, source-cited briefs, contradiction detection, $99 single-deal
   trial, "an analyst-day a month" pricing), **Searcher OS** and **DealOrb** (deal sourcing + AI CIM
   analysis + DD task management for self-funded searchers), **Bizzed** (free AI DD report from as
   little as a website URL, 24–48h turnaround, monetized via acquisition-financing referrals; full
   launch Q3 2025), and **Acquire.com's** AI task manager (auto-generates a DD checklist at LOI).
   All `EVIDENCE` (direct fetches of dealprint.io, devaland.com, bizzed.xyz) or `SECONDARY`
   (searcheros.ai, dealorb.ai, acquire.com blog via search). **Every one is financial/document-
   centric and buyer-facing. None does structured technology/website assessment; none sells to
   consultants; none offers whitelabel.**
3. **Finsider.ai went from quiet analog to loud public launch.** GlobeNewswire releases (Sept 25 and
   Sept 29, 2025) announce its QoE automation platform: "74-point automated scan," "100% transaction
   coverage," QuickBooks/Xero/NetSuite integrations, "60% faster," explicitly targeting transaction
   advisory firms, CPAs, and fractional CFOs. `EVIDENCE` (press releases). It also now runs a
   content-marketing blog ranking M&A software — it is actively marketing to CloseCheck's exact
   buyer. Still financial-only; no technology-DD scope found.
4. **Institutional AI-DD tooling is now a settled, expensive category — and confirmed out of
   segment.** Harvey, Hebbia, Kira/Litera, Luminance, Keye, Robin AI, Diligen, DealRoom AI,
   Intralinks DealCentre AI, Imprima Smart VDR: all legal/financial/VDR scope at $10k–$200k+/yr,
   targeting PE, corporate development, and law firms. A 10-vendor 2026 comparison's own summary:
   "No vendor specializes in technology/website assessment or small-business consultant
   acquisitions." `SECONDARY` (ctacquisitions.com roundup). Cheapest named down-market entries:
   Diligen ($10–30k/yr, search funds) and Robin AI ($20–60k/yr, independent sponsors) — legal DD
   only. Deloitte 2025: 86% of corporate/PE leaders use GenAI in M&A workflows, 35% of those in DD.
5. **A substitute the baseline missed: MSP IT-assessment tooling.** RapidFire Tools **Network
   Detective Pro** (Kaseya) is existing, mature software that runs automated network/security/M365
   scans and generates **fully brandable (whitelabel) IT assessment reports**, sold by subscription
   (~$199–299/mo per 2020 promo pricing; current pricing quote-based) to thousands of MSPs and IT
   consultants — including explicitly for prospecting and "new revenue-generating services."
   `EVIDENCE` (rapidfiretools.com) + `SECONDARY` (pricing history). This is the closest *shipped
   software* analog to CloseCheck's Tier A + whitelabel report anywhere in the landscape — built for
   recurring managed-services sales, not deal-triggered diligence, and its scans require network
   access (a Tier-C-like posture CloseCheck deliberately avoids in v1).
6. **Whitelabel website-audit report tooling is commodity-cheap.** MetricSpot, Auditzy, WebCEO,
   Serpstat, RoboAuditor et al. sell branded website/SEO audit PDFs to agencies for tens of dollars
   a month, some with client portals. `SECONDARY`. None has any M&A/diligence framing, but they cap
   what "automated website scan → branded PDF" alone can be worth.
7. **The service layer thickened but did not productize.** AuditCraft is unchanged — same
   $2,500/$4,500 fixed tiers, $500K–$10M deals, 5/7-day delivery, no software, no whitelabel
   (`EVIDENCE`, auditcraft.org 2026-09-21). New/confirmed service names in the online-business
   niche: **VeryDiligent** (fixed-fee tech DD, €10k–50k+, SaaS/software targets — above CloseCheck's
   deal band), **Centurica** ($500K–$50M online-business DD, 3–4 week turnarounds, free
   "Marketwatch" listing aggregator but no DD software), **Rapid Diligence** (QoE for $200–800k
   online deals — still no tech-review SKU, consistent with the baseline's parent research),
   **WebAcquisition** and **Jaded** (Flippa DD partners; Jaded is tech+financial for software
   acquisitions). **Flippa** now runs automatic DD on listings above $50k, paid deep-dive DD reports
   ($500–$2,000), verified financial integrations (Stripe/Shopify/QuickBooks/GA), and a DD partner
   directory. `EVIDENCE` (flippa.com partner page) + `SECONDARY` (pricing).
8. **ChatGPT/Claude ad-hoc DD is now a normalized substitute**, with prompt packs, AI-generated
   checklists, and explicit third-party guidance that "for small firms, general-purpose assistants
   handle the widest range of DD tasks at the lowest cost." `SECONDARY`.

**Not re-verified this pass** (carried from baseline, no contrary evidence found): Content
Snare/SuiteDash/Assembly and Karbon/Financial Cents/Canopy pricing; QoE $8–15k anchors; Ansarada
floor. One correction candidate: Midaxo was `BLOCKED` in the baseline; third-party 2026 roundups now
place DealRoom/Midaxo/Devensoft at roughly **$10k–40k/yr for a lower-middle-market team**
(`SECONDARY`) — still an order of magnitude above what a solo consultant would pay, so the
"enterprise, out of segment" conclusion stands with better pricing evidence.

## 3. Does the baseline's gap thesis still hold?

**Yes, in its narrow form — verbatim.** After a materially broader search pass than the baseline's
(full WebSearch, 2026 vendor roundups, the AI-entrant wave, the MSP-tooling vertical), still **no
one sells purpose-built technology-DD software — tiered checklist engine + business-type templates
+ whitelabeled findings report + client portal — to independent M&A/QoE consultants for
small-business deals.** A 10-vendor 2026 AI-DD comparison reaches the same conclusion in its own
words. The three-legged structure of the baseline's §3 gap argument survives intact: service demand
is still real and priced (AuditCraft unchanged; Flippa $500–2k reports; DueDilio tech-DD category),
no vendor has crossed from doing tech-DD work to selling the tool, and generic tools still cover
the generic half cheaply.

**But the gap is narrowing from three directions, and two are new since 2026-09-11:**

1. **The report-generation layer is commoditizing fastest.** Bizzed produces a free AI DD report
   from a URL; Hebbia/Harvey-class tools auto-draft diligence memos upmarket; CENTRL-style
   configurable report builders exist. **Tier A alone — automated checks rendered into a PDF — is no
   longer a durable moat and should not be positioned as one.** What AI report generators do *not*
   erode: Tier B's structured evidence capture from a specific seller, Tier C's consultant-attested
   findings (an accountability/liability artifact an LLM cannot supply), the business-type checklist
   taxonomy, and the consultant-branded delivery workflow. The moat claim should be restated as
   Tiers B+C + templates + whitelabel workflow, with Tier A as the demo/wedge.
2. **DueDilio's software capability is now demonstrated, not hypothetical.** The distribution-risk
   clock (baseline §4) is ticking faster.
3. **RapidFire proves the exact business model in an adjacent vertical** — consultants already pay
   ~$200–300/mo for automated-assessment + whitelabel-report software. This is double-edged: it
   validates CloseCheck's price point above the $19–215/mo generic-portal ceiling the baseline
   worried about, and it names a well-resourced vendor one positioning pivot away.

**Net assessment:** the competitive gap conclusion stands; the *durability* estimate should be
revised down. The baseline implied the empty space was structural and stable ("the empty parts look
structural, not undiscovered"). This pass shows the surrounding spaces filling in at 2025–2026 AI
speed — buyer-side AI diligence, financial-DD-consultant software, marketplace DD productization —
with CloseCheck's specific cell still empty. The window is real but should be treated as
12–24 months, not indefinite. The baseline's recommended next step is unchanged and more urgent:
willingness-to-pay conversations with 10–15 DueDilio/AuditCraft-adjacent providers beat further
competitor search.
