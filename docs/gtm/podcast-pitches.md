# Podcast guest pitches

Drafted 2026-09-22 per `docs/research/promotion.md` §1 and channel ranking #4: **guest first,
sponsor only after guesting proves the message** (sponsorship ~$1–2.5k/ep, gated on 3–5 nameable
customers per the 90-day sequence). Pitch in month 1; expect airing months 2–3.

Shared rules: the story is the *diligence gap*, not the product — hosts allergic to vendor pitches
will take a guest who brings teardown material. Zero-customer honesty: "pre-revenue, recruiting
design partners" said plainly if asked. Consultant framing throughout: the audience hears "ask
your QoE provider about this," never "buy this instead of hiring a consultant."

## 1. Acquiring Minds (Will Smith)

**Why this show:** 495 episodes of founder-buys-business interviews; the audience is searchers
mid-journey — exactly the people whose consultants we sell to. Format favors narrative and
post-close hindsight.

**Personalization:** reference specific episodes where tech surfaced post-close (there are
several: acquirers discovering the website/booking stack wasn't what they thought — pick 1–2
recent ones by name in the actual email; slot: `{{episode_ref}}`). Angle the pitch as "the
segment of your interviews where the buyer says 'what I wish I'd checked' — I've got the
checklist version of that segment."

**Proposed angle:** *"The diligence nobody's QoE covers: what buyers of main-street businesses
find out at handover."*

**Five talking points:**
1. The handover horror pattern: seller's nephew owns the domain, the Google Business Profile is
   tied to a personal Gmail, the POS contract dies on transfer — real patterns from teardown work,
   with the sample-audit findings as concrete examples.
2. Why financial QoE structurally misses this: scope, not competence — earnings quality vs. asset
   control are different questions, and nobody's engagement letter covers the second.
3. The 20-minute public-records check any buyer can demand before LOI: WHOIS/RDAP domain ownership,
   DNS control, SSL, email authentication (will payroll and invoices even send after transfer?),
   platform fingerprinting.
4. What belongs in the purchase agreement because of it: domain/account transfer schedules,
   platform-contract assignability, credential handover as a closing condition.
5. Where the line is between what software can check (public records) and what needs a human with
   judgment (contracts, seller attestations) — honest about both, which is also the product's
   actual tier structure without pitching it.

## 2. Acquisitions Anonymous (Girdley, D'Alessandro, Snell, Endresen)

**Why this show:** deal-teardown format — hosts pull apart a real listing on air. A tech teardown
of a live listing is *native content*, not an interview ask. They actively carry sponsors, so a
later sponsorship path exists if guesting lands.

**Personalization:** the show mocks bad listings lovingly; the pitch offers ammunition. Reference
a recent teardown episode (`{{episode_ref}}`) and offer: "next time you tear down a listing, I'll
run the tech layer on it live-ish — you'll have the WHOIS, the stack, the email-auth posture on
screen while you talk margins."

**Proposed angle:** *"Same teardown you already do — plus the layer where the domain, the reviews,
and the booking stack turn out not to be included in the sale."*

**Five talking points:**
1. Live teardown of a listing the hosts pick (prepared in advance): what the public record says vs.
   what the broker listing claims — the gap is the entertainment.
2. "The revenue runs through rented pipes": listings whose sales are actually DoorDash/Booksy/
   Shopify relationships with non-obvious transfer terms — how to spot it from outside.
3. The five-figure findings that cost $0 to check: expired-domain-adjacent risks, email domain on a
   blacklist, SSL/HTTPS hygiene as a proxy for general neglect.
4. What Girdley's "hairy deal" instinct looks like as a checklist — codifying the sniff test for
   the tech layer of main-street deals.
5. For the advisor listeners specifically: how a QoE or DD shop adds this as a line item without
   hiring an engineer — the methodology-not-headcount point (the product exists behind this point;
   name it once, move on).

## Founder bio (for both pitches / show notes)

> Sergii Prykhozhai is the founder of CloseCheck, software that packages technology due diligence
> for main-street acquisitions — restaurants, salons, franchises, e-commerce — into a tiered,
> repeatable audit that M&A and QoE consultants deliver under their own brand. He built it after
> research showed the sub-$2M deal market has diligence coverage for earnings but almost none for
> the domains, platforms, and contracts the earnings actually run through. CloseCheck is
> pre-revenue and working with its first design partners.
